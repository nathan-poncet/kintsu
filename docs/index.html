In one glance

What Kintsu is. What it is not.

  • A hook and a small resident daemon in the shell you already use.
  • A bubble under the failure: one seam, one sentence, a line of words you can click.
  • Rules first, models when needed, your agent for the real work.
  • Your models and your keys: any provider, local when you want, local when it must.
  • Quiet by default: nothing on success, one bubble per failure, mute in one word.
  • Open source, MIT, one static binary.
  • Not a terminal emulator, not a shell, not a PTY wrapper.
  • Not a chat TUI: if you want to chat, it hands you to one.
  • Not autocomplete: zsh-autosuggestions and friends do that.
  • Not something that runs commands for you: you press Enter, always.
  • Not a service: no account, no telemetry, no server of ours.
  • Not finished: pre-alpha, the design is done, the bubble comes with v0.1.

Questions

The ones everyone asks first.

Does Kintsu ever run a command?

No. Never. A fix is inserted in your line editor; you press Enter. In the panel it takes two Enters on purpose. Destructive patterns are red first.

What leaves my machine, and when?

Nothing until you pick an action that needs a model. Then the redacted case: command, status, output, cwd, git state, the last ten commands. Never your files, never your environment. The Privacy tab shows the exact payload.

Can I use it fully offline?

Yes. Rules need nothing; with Ollama, fixes and explanations run on your machine. A secret spotted in the output forces local-only for that case anyway.

Isn't this Warp?

Warp did it first, inside Warp, with a Warp account and Warp's models. Kintsu does it in the terminal and shell you already use, with any model or agent, keys in your keychain, source on GitHub.

Can I use my Claude or ChatGPT subscription instead of a key?

Yes, through the CLI you already have: Claude Code, Codex, Copilot CLI or Gemini CLI as the agent. Kintsu writes the brief and launches the CLI; the CLI uses its own login.

Which terminals make the bubble clickable?

iTerm2, WezTerm, Kitty, Ghostty, foot, VS Code, Windows Terminal, GNOME Terminal and other VTE terminals render the links. Elsewhere the words still show and the keys still work.
